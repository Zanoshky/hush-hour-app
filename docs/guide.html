How Hush Hour works

Start in one tap

Hush Hour opens on Sounds. Press the round play key under the dial. The first time, soft rain on the roof plays. The dial says what is going on (Ready, Time left, Fading out or All night), how long is left, and the clock time it goes silent. The line under it names what plays.

Three sections in the bar

The bar at the foot of the screen has three sections: Sounds, Mixes and Settings. The one you are on sits on a blue pill. Tap another to switch; it opens at its top. Tap the one you are on to scroll back to its top. Back on Mixes or Settings takes you to Sounds.

At the top right are two keys on every section: the moon dims the screen, and the three dots open More.

Sounds

Scroll down on Sounds to the sound tiles. Tap a tile to add its sound to the mix, tap it again to take it out. A tile that plays is lit in moonlight, with a bar along its foot that shows its level. You can play up to eight sounds at once.

Rain
Rain on a yard, rain on the glass (Window), on the roof, on a tent, a mountain downpour, and a storm with far thunder now and then.
Water
Waves on the shore (Ocean) and a river murmur (Stream).
Woods and night
Birds in the trees (Forest), wind in the leaves, and crickets on a summer night with an owl now and then.
Fire
A campfire and a fireplace, with a log popping now and then.
Noise
A bedroom fan, and white, pink and brown noise. White is bright like a hiss, pink is softer like steady rain, brown is deep and low.

Rare moments (thunder, the owl, a pop in the fire) can be turned off in Settings, Sound.

Your mix

Under the tiles, Your mix lists each sound that plays with its own slider, its level, and a cross to take it out. Overall level sets the whole mix. Keep it low: soft enough that you could talk over it.

Save this mix, at the foot of the card, takes you to Mixes with the name box ready.

Mixes

At the top of Mixes, Save what plays now: type a name, like Sunday rain, and tap Save. Your mixes show under Yours, with a cross to delete each one. Under Ready made are nine to start from: Rainy cabin, Summer night, Campfire, Storm at sea, Tent in the rain, Window seat, Shore at dawn, Deep focus and Soft hush.

Tap a mix to play it. Hush Hour plays it and takes you back to Sounds. The mix that plays says Playing now, in moonlight.

The sleep timer

The chips under the dial set the timer: 15 or 30 minutes, 45 (the default), 1 hour, 1 hour 30, 2 hours, 2 hours 30, or All night. The chosen chip is blue with a small tick. The timer starts when you press play. Near the end the sound fades out slowly, over 10 minutes by default; pick 5 or 20 in Settings, Sleep timer.

Why a timer by default? Steady sound helps most while you fall asleep, and one study found noise left on all night took some dream sleep. All night is there if you want it.

More

The three dots at the top open More, a sheet from the foot of the screen. It names what plays, then holds Save this mix and Add the widget.

Dim the screen

Tap the moon at the top. The screen goes black with only the time, in a dim grey, and the brightness drops to the lowest. Tap anywhere, or press Back, to wake it.

With the screen off

Sound keeps playing with the screen off. The notification and the lock screen show what plays, with pause and stop. A call or an alarm pauses it, and it comes back after. If headphones come out, it pauses so the sound never jumps to the speaker (you can turn that off in Settings, Sound). To play under a podcast or audiobook, turn on Play along with other apps.

The widget

Pick Add the widget in More or in Settings, Sleep and sound. Or touch and hold your home screen, pick Widgets, then Hush Hour. The widget shows the mix and plays or pauses it with one tap. Tap its words to open the app.

Settings

Settings is the third item in the bar. It is a page of cards, each row saying what is set now; a row with an arrow opens its own small page, and the arrow at the top left of that page takes you back.

Accessibility

Every control has a TalkBack label, and each sound tile is read as a switch that is on or off. Touch targets are at least 48 dp, text grows with the phone's font size (the tiles get wider as it does, one across at the biggest sizes), and words keep a 7 to 1 contrast in every look.
